## 1. `543` — the run's own rejects reach the dataset

`rec["class"]` is fed from `merged`, which is `classify_grouped`'s ACCEPTED output, so a role
the seam re-judged NO was never in it: the cell kept yesterday's `accept` and the dataset
published a false accept for the rest of the 90-day window. The only correction was a human
writing a line — **32 of the 48 lines in `roles_retractions.jsonl` were written by hand
between 09-01 and 09-11**.

`roles.reject_map(candidates + gcands)` reads the `_class` already stamped on every judged
copy. `run.py` hands it to `record_run(class_rejects=…)`; `_record_run` applies it after the
status ladder and before the backfill map. Three refusals, each a rule and not a precaution:

| refusal | why it is not optional |
|---|---|
| `rid in by_key` | a mixed group puts one role_id in both the map and `merged`; the reject would contradict the board the same run renders |
| status not in `open`/`closed` | a withdrawn or purged cell is a HUMAN's adjudication; a superseded record is not published at all |
| the status is not changed | `_alive` is the liveness rule. A reject is never upserted, so `last_seen` stops moving and the record closes tomorrow on the ordinary ladder, inside the mass-close guard |

That last one is the decision I expect to be questioned, so the reasoning is on the record:
closing the row here would (a) contradict the board this same run renders — the role is still
in `onboard` for one more morning on yesterday's `last_seen`, (b) skip the mass-close guard,
and (c) misuse `closed`, which the column vocabulary defines as *the posting was gone from the
board*. It is not gone; it is not ours. The cost is at most one morning of `status=open` with
`class_decision=reject`, which is the shape `Parametrix` has shipped since 09-02.

**A guard I added after writing the feature**, because the failure mode is asymmetric: a
KEYWORD-rule change never enters `Classifier.quarantine()` (that reads the LLM tier only), so
a scope edit could flip many open rows in one morning with nothing to say so. `roles
mass-reject (N of M open roles re-judged NO in one run)` is an ALARM and deliberately not a
hold — holding would republish the very accepts the item exists to stop.

The quarantine discard sits **outside** the backfill's `use_llm and not (only or limit)` gate
and outside its `try`: a scoped LLM run can quarantine too, and the backfill's `except` would
otherwise swallow the reject map's discard silently.

## 2. `544` — the verdict says which contract made it

The classifier lane shipped the seam half in the same hour (their `base` dicts, their
hand-built backfill rejects, and a 5th element on `_lookup` naming the prefix that actually
answered, so a superseded cache hit reports the retired hash rather than nothing). Mine is one

string-matching `reason` for `(superseded contract)`. Reason strings are the classifier lane's
prose and one 09-02 commit rewrote 13 of them; a hash inferred from a sentence and published

and an honest one.

Export: `class_contract` (57 → 58 columns) and a `classifier_contract` meta block
(`live`/`rows_current`/`rows_stale`/`rows_unknown`, which sum to the file). What it makes
visible: **38 of 262** records are `closed` + `accept`, frozen under whatever contract judged
them on the day they closed, because a closed role never re-enters `merged`. **Nothing drains
them** — this column makes the freeze legible, it does not fix it, and the report says so.

## 3. `579` — a card blob is not a title

`We're Hiring Junior Web Analyst - Practical Vision` and `We’re Hiring Web Analyst - Practical
Vision` were on the board and in the mail. The title is half of `merge_key`, so the blob is
half of the role_id.

I checked whether the existing fold arms could already do this before writing a new one, and
they cannot: for the Practical Vision pair `same_role_twin`, `same_posting` and `_titles_agree`
all return False (the prefix rule fails, the `_pk` tail `junior-web-analyst` is not id-shaped
so the seniority bypass is off), and the old record is not in `merged` anyway.

`canonical_title` cuts three things, each refusing rather than guessing. Measured over all 262
records and both caches: **5 rename, 0 fold, 0 left**. The kept cases are the point —
`- Temporary position`, `- Marketing`, `- Payments`, `, Growth`, `| Corporate Banking Division
Headquarters 3103`, `| SQL & Power BI`, and `We’re Hiring` alone at sensi (cutting the call
would leave nothing, so the card keeps its blob and the render keeps hiding it).

The brief described `ת/יקסע תי/טסילנא ה/שורד` as a reversed-Hebrew blob. It is not: the stored
title is `דרוש/ה אנליסט/ית עסקי/ת – BI & Data` in logical order, and the reversal was the
operator's terminal. I searched every store for reversed tokens and found none —
`scrape_universal.py` strips bidi marks at capture. The real class is the Hebrew hiring call
`דרוש/ה`, which is the same class as `We're Hiring`, and is cut by the same rule.

**The rename is the load-bearing part, and "leave it in place" was not available.** The alias
sweep declines to rename when it finds no twin; a title canon cannot, because the canonical
key now arrives from intake every morning — an uncanonical record would stop being fed, close
as if the posting had gone, and reappear as a new record with today's `first_seen`. One
posting, published twice, with a false closure and a false "new". So `fold_titles` renames
across all three stores in one seam, or folds into a live twin through `_fold_into_twin`.

Three things I got to by breaking them first:

1. **The shrink guard compares KEY SETS, not counts.** A rename keeps 262 records and still
   loses a key, so the ledger write is refused — every morning, while sqlite is already
   rekeyed. `may_drop` is the sanctioned channel and both dumps now declare the rename through
   it. The guard is unchanged for anything nobody renamed, which
   `test_a_rename_never_trips_the_shrink_guard_but_a_bare_substitution_still_does` pins.
2. **`roles_text.jsonl` joins on `role_id`.** A rename that leaves the text line behind
   orphans the description, and `flush`'s orphan prune then DELETES it — losing text a paid
   fetch bought and publishing the role as description-less.
3. **`_fold_into_twin` exists because I duplicated it first.** My twin branch was a copy of
   the alias sweep's, and `test_no_mutation_record_goes_stale_unnoticed` went red: the
   `alias-sweep-drops-the-sid-union` record's anchor now matched TWO places. The honest fix
   was one seam rather than a re-anchor with more context, and the mutation record now covers
   both sweeps. That test earned its keep today.

## 4. `580` — a posting whose own page says it is closed

`migdal|business analyst` was on the board for 17 days and in the 08-26 email while the page
we stored for it said `כבר לא מקבלים בקשות` at character 264 of 4,523. **4 of the 44 open
LinkedIn-only records** are in that state, plus one already purged.

The cause is the cache, not the text: a discovery card is carried forward for 21 days by
`posted_date`, so `last_seen` moves every morning and `_alive`'s "we saw it in the latest scan"
is satisfied by OUR memory of the card rather than by LinkedIn. Nothing read the page.

`page_closed` is narrow on purpose: the posting's own LinkedIn address, EVERY source a
`discovery-*` one (a role the employer's own board still lists is open whatever a 21-day-stale
mirror says), and the marker inside the first 600 characters, where the chrome puts it. Plus
two arms that are about durability rather than detection: jd-text's `closed-by-page` stamp
(filed as `582@jd-text` and agreed with that session), and a url-bound ledger memory so a
later chrome strip cannot silently reopen all four, while a posting re-listed at a NEW address
reopens on the ordinary ladder.

**`closed_on` is the day the text was captured, never today.** A reader takes that column for
the day the posting went away, and today would claim we watched it close. Today's four:
Migdal `2026-08-28`, One datAI `2026-08-30`, Mizrahi Tefahot `2026-09-01`, Computer Guard
`2026-09-04`.

**The churn I found by tracing it rather than by running it:** without also skipping the
upsert, `closed_keys()` reads the re-served card as a reappearance, `keep_first` goes False,
`first_seen` resets to today and a fresh episode is minted — a daily `reopened 1` and a role
that keeps re-entering the 48h email window, for up to 21 days. `run.py` now skips the upsert
for a page-closed row, which freezes `last_seen` at the honest value.

## 6. What I did not finish, and what I made harder for the next lane

* **Nothing here is proven unattended yet.** Four morning-check rows come due 2026-09-12 and
  the lane's number in `docs/AGENT_BRIEF.md` reads 4/5/38 until they answer, not 0/0/38.
* **The 38 frozen verdicts are legible, not drained.** Draining them means re-judging a closed
  row, which is `classifier`'s `judge_backfill` and reaches only EMPTY cells today.
* **Harder for the next lane, stated plainly:** a `role_id` can now change. It was previously
  a fixed string for the life of a record, and four things depend on that — `roles_text.jsonl`
  joins on it, a retraction line may name it, `LedgerShrink` reads a lost key as a deletion,
  and the public dataset publishes it. All four are handled (`renamed_from`, `may_drop`, the
  text move, the meta's `store.renamed`), but anyone adding a FIFTH consumer of `role_id` must
  now ask whether it survives a rename. `registry`'s incoming `_alias_fold_target` change
  touches the same file; I sent them my exact hunks and neither of us edits the other's.
* **`582@jd-text`** is filed and agreed, not done: until jd-text stamps `closed-by-page`, the
  text arm of `page_closed` depends on chrome nobody has promised to preserve.
* **The alias sweep's foldable-with-no-twin branch still declines to rename** (measured empty
  today). The primitive now exists if it stops being empty.
