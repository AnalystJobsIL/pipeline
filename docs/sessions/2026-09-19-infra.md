`health.record` has written `nights` since 2026-09-18 and the MAIL waits for

**One predicate, not the filed copy in two files.** `pipeline/health.one_night_reading` sits
beside `_nights_of` and `REGRESSION_NIGHTS` in the module that WRITES the field, and both
consumers import it — `resolve_broken` already imported `ATS_HOST` from there with the comment

value with a docstring explaining why, so 99 was not added: **two spellings of one default in
three files is how the `_load_secrets` copies happened** (`468`).

Two classes are skipped at `nights 1` and no others:

* `regressed-to-zero` — a board that produced yesterday and produces nothing today is the claim
  `REGRESSION_NIGHTS` exists to make, and one night is not that claim;
* `fetch-error` whose recorded text contains `network error` — a socket that never answered says
  nothing about the ADDRESS, which is the only thing a resolver can change. The six
  SuccessFactors rows that timed out on 2026-09-18 are this shape and cleared next night 5 of 5
  times in the fortnight before.

Everything else is taken on its first night and always was: `empty-board` and
`misconfig-scrape-on-ats` are readings of a SHAPE, where a second night says nothing new;
`abandoned-board` is date arithmetic; and a `fetch-error` that is an HTTP STATUS
(`scrape: http:403 (19 nights)`) is the board ANSWERING, which is exactly the evidence a
resolver acts on. That last split is why the record `stale-transient-error-word-dropped` exists.

**Measured first, on origin's file.** `cloud_state/stale.json` holds **109** entries; **0** carry
`nights` (`health`'s writer landed 348811e and writes from the next digest on), so the change
