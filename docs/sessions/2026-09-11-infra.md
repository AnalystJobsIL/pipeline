# 2026-09-11 — `infra`: the ceiling bound one spender of fourteen, and it was the wrong one

Four items: the operator's Bright Data ruling (A), `577`'s one token (B), three guards that
rot on the calendar (C), and the cache-shrink threshold the 2026-09-11 morning check asked
for (D). Every number below was re-derived here from committed state, from `gh run view
--log`, or from the live account's metadata endpoints; where the spawn brief's number differs
it is corrected and the correction is marked.

**Credits spent: 0.** Every measurement is committed state or a run log. The one process that
called the account (`python -m pipeline.bd_budget`) reads `datasets/v3/snapshots` and
`zone/cost`, which bill nothing, and from a worktree it returns `unknown` anyway.

## 1. What the ceiling actually did

`Bright Data: 5,804 of 5,000 credits (116%) — CEILING REACHED` on the 11th of the month. Only
two modules consult `bd_budget.verdict()`, so the ONE consumer it stopped was the digest's JD
fill: 49 postings judged on their titles alone, 10 superseded classifier verdicts that could

The operator's ruling: *"unlimited budget for now; optimize once, then let it drive itself."*
Encoded in `bd_budget.ceiling()` with a date, like the 08-28 rule it supersedes, and the

09-11 unlimited → stays). 5,000 survives as `SOFT`, because it is the FREE TIER and past it a
credit is $1.50/1,000: this September is about $15, not a wall.

## 2. The waste, and why the fix is a STORE and not a schedule

The ledger, 09-01..09-11 (5,375 credits): `listing_hunt` 1,741 · `bd_rescue` 1,032 ·
`crack_walled` 847 · `queue_resolve_search` 658 · `resolve_broken` 463 · `discovery_daily` 231
· `audit_empty_rows` 155 · six others 248. By purpose: search 76%, unlock 19%, discovery 4%.

**The cadence already existed and the notes column was eating it.** `listing_hunt.stale_hunt`
has said 14 days since 08-21. Measured: of the 93 rows the hunt would take that night, **80
carried no `listing-hunt` stamp at all**, and 62 of those had notes of 160-220 characters.
`notes.append` evicts the oldest unprotected segment to fit 220 chars and twelve tools write
into that cell. An evicted stamp reads as *never hunted* and sorts FIRST. The 617-row pool was
being re-walked every 3-4 nights (178 / 272 / 252 rows on 09-10 / 09-09 / 09-08).

`bd_rescue` had the same defect by another door: its commonest outcome, `validated`, writes
`scanned via brightdata`, which carries **no date**, so the 7-day cooldown keyed on `bd-tried`
never applied to 15 of its 17 rows — `rescued 0 · validated 16-18` at 80-95 credits, four
nights running. `crack_walled` was daily by design (`_recrackable(note, days=1)`): 847 credits
for 2-3 `cracked-api` a night out of 74 rows.

**Three lanes of evidence, one conclusion: a schedule does not belong in a 220-character cell
shared by twelve writers.** It moved to `cloud_state/queue_state.json`, which already dates an
attempt per NAME per RUNG, under one predicate composed from the intake queue's own
(`tried_within`):

> `queue_state.row_due(state, name, rung, days=14, url=...)` — due when the rung has never
> tried the row, when its last attempt is older than `days`, or when the row's ADDRESS changed
> since that attempt.

`actionable_mode` is composed with it and now compares a triage mode against **our last
attempt** rather than against the stamp — the same bug in the second predicate, and the reason
the first fix did not hold when I measured it (the guard failed, which is what guards are for).

**Rejected, on the measurement:** protecting the `listing-hunt` segment in `pipeline/notes.py`
the way `dark-triage` is protected. 62 of the 80 affected rows are AT the cap, so one more
protected segment makes `append` drop the NEWCOMER — the newest verdict, from whichever tool
ran last.

**Seeded, not restarted.** With an empty log every row is "never tried" and night one re-buys
the whole pool. The dated evidence that SURVIVED is real evidence, so 537 + 66 + 17 attempts
were seeded from the surviving note stamps and from the 02:30 run log's own `empt` lines
(run 34574145122, 16 names). After seeding: hunt 619 → **95** due tonight (85 on the clock, 10
on a mode), crack 74 → **8**, rescue 17 → **0** (next due 09-25).

**Projection: 468 credits/day → ~225.** The brief hoped for 3,000-4,000/month; that is not
reachable by cadence, because what remains is the search rung itself. Said plainly in the
decision record rather than rounded towards the brief.

`queue_resolve_search` was NOT touched: of the 84 names it searched on 09-10, every one had
exactly ONE `search-llm` attempt ever (690 of 755 queue names do). Its cadence works, because
a NAME has no notes cell to evict.

## 3. The gauge, and two things that were invisible

`bd:` on `Stage order:` — month-to-date from the live account, the 7-day rate per purpose, the
month's projection — and a `Stages:` clause past `SOFT` with the PAYG dollars. It alarms and
refuses nothing, which is the ruling in one behaviour.

For that to be true, two facts had to become visible:

* **What a credit was for.** The ledger records the SCRIPT (`os.path.basename(sys.argv[0])`)
  and twelve scripts buy the same Google SERP. `bd_rescue.book(purpose)` labels each credit
  (default `unlock`, so every existing caller is byte-identical) and the line carries the map.
  Lines written before today have no map; `TOOL_PURPOSE` maps them by tool, and the reader
  handles both shapes for ever.
* **That the digest spends at all.** `jdfill.Unlocker` POSTs to `api.brightdata.com` itself
  and had never written a ledger line. That is a large part of why the account read 5,804 and
  the ledger 5,375.

**The account and the ledger disagree for a legitimate reason and the record says so**: the
account counts PRODUCTS (`unlocker_reqs=3378, serp_reqs=2308, dataset_records=118`) and the
ledger counts CALLERS. A search tool spends one SERP credit and then unlocks the pages it
found, so it lands in both columns. Never add them.

**A defect this lane found in itself.** A throwaway `python -c` calling `book()` twice
appended `{"credits":3,...,"tool":"-c"}` to the TRACKED ledger — three credits nobody spent,
in the artefact whose whole purpose is to be believed later, and which the gauge now reads
back. `pytest in sys.modules` did not cover it because it was not pytest. A credit cannot be
bought without a credential, so a checkout with no key now writes no line at all. Reverted the
phantom line; the guard is `test_a_credit_nobody_could_have_bought_is_never_written_to_the_ledger`.

## 4. The reserve: built, proven at the seam, off

`BD_ALLOWANCES=1` makes `may_spend(purpose)` enforce search 2,000 / jd-fill 1,500 /
discovery 800 / unlock 700 (sums to `SOFT`; a test pins that). `jd-fill` is privileged: it
borrows every other class's unspent remainder before it is refused, because a role published
with no description is a defect in the product and a company re-checked a week late is not.

The split deliberately does NOT copy the measured shares — a description of today is not a
budget. Shipped off: there is no ceiling to enforce, and an allowance nobody asked for is a
silent coverage cut waiting for a busy night.

The seam is the part worth having: `unlock_status` consults it before building the request;
`jdfill` and `bd_employees`, the two modules that POST themselves, consult it in one line
each. The guard is parametrised over **eighteen** spenders and follows the IMPORT GRAPH —
`repair_extract_gap` buys through `scrape_universal` and `auto_expand` through the gate and
the drain, and a grep-shaped test would have called both of them unbudgeted and sent the next
session to add a seam that is already there.

## 5. The search rung: one free rung, measured before it is trusted

76% of the month is `google_via_unlocker`. Every vendor alternative was priced against
$1.50/1,000 and rejected on the number (the decision record carries the table; the research is
`bd_alternatives_research.txt`). What shipped instead:

* **DuckDuckGo reads its own throttle.** Its soft block is HTTP **202** with "Ratelimit" —
  not a 4xx, not an empty page — so a fetcher that reads only the body records "no search
  results", a claim about the company made by a rate limit. One 202 now ends the free rung for
  the run; 2 s pacing; `Israel` in the query for the same reason `gl=il` is on the Google URL.
* **Both rungs rank identically.** `_rank_hosts` was extracted from the paid rung (jobs-ish
  path > any path > bare host, shortest wins). `ddg` had been answering in raw document order,
  which is not a substitute when the caller renders `cands[:2]`.
* **It goes first in the two tools that had no free rung**: `queue_resolve_search` (12% of the
  month) and `resolve_broken` (9%).
* **And it is measured, free**: every paid search also asks DDG and prints
  `[search-ab] <name> ddg=<host> unlocker=<host> agree=y/n`, with a summary at exit. **Keep
  the rung at ≥ 70% agreement; below that the next session deletes it and says so.** That is
  the 09-12 morning check.

**LinkedIn.** The guest walk fired up to 50 back-to-back requests per query with no delay —
the free rung buying the paid one. Spaced at 2.5 s, with one paced re-ask of a hard block
before the credit. One shared `_absorb()` keeps the recovered page deduped exactly as the main
path does; my first draft deduped on `url` where the walk dedupes on `job_id`.

**The dataset trigger was live and re-arms itself every month.** It bills 1 credit per RECORD

would arm again. Now behind `LINKEDIN_TARGETED=1`, with the zero still recorded so `last_run`
does not read a disarmed source as a dead one. `budget_per_day` also reads `bd_budget.SOFT`
now: there were two numbers for the free tier and only one of them moved when the operator

## 7. Three guards that rot on the calendar

Two carried a literal `posted_date`/`created` inside the 21-day intake window and went red on
09-10 — *a guard that fails on a date tested nothing in the weeks before it went red*. The
third passed `today=2026-09-05` into `archive_evidence.run` while `_now()` stamped the ledger
with the WALL clock, so `_days(today, at)` went negative and the pending line stopped being
selected for verification.

The window itself was **five** copies of `(date.today() - 21 days)` in `discovery_daily` plus
a **sixth** in `pipeline/fetchers` — the module that READS the cache those five WRITE, so a
change to the window would have applied to the writers and not to the reader. One `FRESH_DAYS`,
one `fresh_cut()`.

**The class, not just the three.** A test that exercises the normalizers or the cache prune
may not carry a literal date the window can walk past; `_days_ago(n)` is how to write one, and
a pre-2021 literal is fine because nothing can make it fresh again. The scan lives INSIDE a
behavioural parametrised guard rather than beside it, because `tools/guard_kill.py` marks a
case that can only pass as CANNOT-FAIL. It immediately found three more fixtures that were
four days from rotting the same way — asserting that a card the prune correctly drops
survives.

## 8. The cache-shrink threshold (§5d, the morning check)

79 commits in `persist_log.jsonl`. `scraped_cache.json`: 37 of them, **5 fires**, largest 36
of 410 (8.8%), rest 3.2-5.6%. Every other keyed cache in the `STRATEGY` table lost nothing at
all in the fortnight. Five alarms in five weeks on the one cache that has been losing boards
is a signal: **kept** at `>= 10 keys AND >= 3%`.

One exemption, about meaning rather than size: `cloud_state/stale.json` fired on **9 of its 18
commits**, up to 41 of 108 (**38%**), and every one was a board HEALING — which is what the
06:00 self-heal exists to produce. An alarm that fires on the good outcome teaches its reader
to skip the line. `SHRINK_EXEMPT`; the delta is still measured, printed and logged.

**And a defect in my own guard for it**: the first draft called `report_deltas(cwd=".")`,
which APPENDED two fabricated lines to the tracked `persist_log.jsonl` — a test mutating the
state file it asserts about. Reverted; the guard runs against `tmp_path`.
