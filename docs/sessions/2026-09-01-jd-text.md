## What shipped — four guards, and what each is honestly worth

**1. A bought pane may not declare a different role** (`_pane_denies_role`, in `fetch_jd`'s
paid rung). The 08-31 rung verified `jobKey == jk` and nothing else, and the jk is the
*discovery card's claim* — that session recorded the binding as an accepted limit. Refusal is
`bd-identity`, definitive, and costs no second credit.

It is **not** `not doc_names_role(...)`: that answers False for everything it merely cannot
confirm, and of the **190** rows the driver walks that pass the bar, **39** carry no strict
mention of their own employer, most of them honest. So it fires only on a positive contradiction — something
was declared, our title has words, both are in the same alphabet, and the title half then
fails the same bar `doc_names_role` sets. The employer is deliberately not part of it: a pane
declaring the right employer and a *different role* is the defect (Diageo's other jk is "VP,
Brands in Culture, NAM"), and requiring both halves to contradict would admit it.

**This does not catch TransUnion or Diageo** — their panes declare the matching role. Said
plainly because the morning check said "TransUnion passes `looks_like_jd`" and it did, while
carrying an Indian pre-sales JD: presence was never identity.

**2. Serialized markup is not a job description** (`_MARKUP_SOUP` in `looks_like_jd`): ≥3
hits of serialization residue in the **first 800 characters**.

Measured over **every card this repo held this morning — 4,684** of them, ~1,860 carrying
any description (203 `matched`, 2,219
`scraped_cache`, 2,262 `discovered_cache`): it fired on **exactly one**, TechBiz (20 hits),
and on nothing else at any window from 400 to 1,500. Re-run tonight, after TechBiz was
refilled from its LinkedIn copy, it fires on **0 of 4,684**: the text it was built for is
gone. Worth stating so a later reader does not take the 0 for a dead rule.

**The window is the rule and the count is not**, and the first draft proves it: ≥8 hits over
`text[:3000]` — the shape a Plan agent proposed on a claimed "0 of 1,316 cache spurious" —
reproduced as **4 real postings vetoed** — four of Fayrix's six cards, whose pages carry a
JSON form-field blob from offset ~2,500 — to catch the same one document. (Three Fattal nav
blobs would also have gone, but they already fail the bar.)

A second group set the THRESHOLD, not the window, and the first draft of this paragraph
conflated the two: ten of Crossriver's fourteen cards and one of Deepdub's carry a literal
`’` in ordinary prose at one or two hits each. They were never near the draft's veto — they
are why the floor is 3 rather than 1. The boundary is stated in the code and pinned by the test's last
assertion: a *short* posting serializing inside the window would be refused; no text here has
that shape, and the cost is a re-fetch, not a deletion.

**3. A text that never names its employer is a candidate, never a verdict**
(`no-company-echo`, a fourth `quality_suspect` suspicion, lowest precedence). Uses
`company_identity.page_mentions_company(strict=True)` — the prose-calibrated primitive, which
wants the name's tokens consecutively. `doc_names_role` is calibrated on ten-word
declarations; over 6,000 characters its employer half degrades to "one company word appears
anywhere", which `TechBiz Global` passes on the word `global`.

It buys one model call and **the model decides** — the only reason it can be generous at
43/197. Employers with no ASCII token are skipped: a Hebrew-named company can never echo in
an ASCII tokenizer, and flagging every Hebrew row for ever is a flood, not a signal.

**4. Only a verdict opens the length ratchet** (`_store_text(..., refuted=True)`).
`_store_text` compares LENGTHS once both sides look like JDs, so **a complete posting
belonging to somebody else is unreplaceable by the shorter true one** — Prisma's 3,276
wrong characters against its own 2,617 correct ones, with no rung in the repo able to install
them. A row the tier read and disowned (`no-company-echo` + rejection) is `refuted`.

Three things keep wave A's donor-overwrite defect shut: only a *job description* may take
the disowned text's place; only the echo suspicion refutes (a row called merely *incomplete*
still defends its own posting); and the donor identity gate is untouched.

## What the adversarial wave found — and it found the guard's own premise false

One Opus wave (money + fake evidence) ran against the committed diff and reproduced six real
defects. **Every one of them was in the half of the change I was most confident about**, and
two would have shipped the exact bug the 08-31 session had already paid for. Each is fixed
here with a test and a mutation record.

**A `partial` verdict refuted, and `partial` means the row's OWN posting.** `jd_quality`
returns `verdict == "complete"`, so `ok is False` covers both "not this role's posting" and
"this role's posting, truncated" — and `_QUALITY_SYSTEM` defines `partial` as *genuine
posting text that stops early*. My branch refuted on the boolean, so a truncated real posting
opened its own length ratchet and a longer different opening could replace it: wave A's
2026-08-31 donor-overwrite defect, reopened from the other side. The wave reproduced it end
to end. Now only a **fresh `not-a-jd`** refutes.

**A cached rejection refuted too, and the cache is a bool.** `llm_cache.verdict` stores 0/1,
so a cache hit cannot say which "no" it was; worse, `verdict` is a loop-carried local, so
reading it on the cached branch serves the PREVIOUS row's class. A cached rejection now
re-queues the row like any other and never unlocks the ratchet. My own plan had said
"fresh-only for the class-sensitive half" and the implementation lost it.

**The refutation outlived the fill.** Nothing discarded an mkey from `refuted` after a rung
filled it, and — this is the part that makes it bite — `cache` and `own-address` donors are
gated on the URL (`_own_posting`), **not** on the document naming the role; only a `copy`
runs `doc_names_role`. So the claim I wrote into §7a — *"the donor identity gate is
untouched, so only text that names THIS role at THIS employer can land in the hole"* — was
**false**. The wave reproduced a 333-character cache donor overwriting a row's own
705-character posting. `refuted.discard` now closes the hole at all three write paths, and
§7a states the narrower invariant that is actually true: the hole is open for ONE write.

**The cross-alphabet guard protected a shape that cannot occur.** `declared_identity`
concatenates the pane's `jobTitle` and `companyName`, so a Hebrew posting at a Latin-named
employer declares in BOTH scripts: my whole-declaration script test passed on the company's
Latin words and then measured a Latin title against Hebrew it could never match. Measured:
`"אנליסט/ית נתונים … Bank Leumi"` vs `Data Analyst` → **denied**, on a board where 25 of 106
Indeed cards carry a Hebrew title. My own test pinned a PURE-Hebrew declaration — a shape
`declared_identity` never builds. The comparison now happens inside the title's own alphabet,
and a declaration whose in-script half is only the employer's name is no evidence at all.

**A refuted row reached the PAID donor rung.** Before, a row with JD-shaped text was skipped
before `_donor_candidates` and could not spend; the wave measured 0 paid calls before and 1
(2 with the render retry) after. Refuted rows are free-only now: a row that already has text
may not preempt one that has none.

**A mutation record no test killed.** `refuted-widens-to-every-suspicion` survived the whole
suite — nothing asserted the CONTENT of `refuted` for a non-echo candidate, so the catalogue
was vouching for a guard that did not exist. That is the failure `540` describes for another
lane's shard, found here in my own.

Two further wave findings are **accepted, not fixed**, and say so: `bd-identity` has no
terminal state (it rides 7/14/28 to the 30-day ceiling and re-buys a pane we have already
proved wrong — the right home is `443`'s per-card reason, unbuilt); and the echo enlarges the
fetch todo from 3 rows to as many as 42, with the new rows sorting FIRST because they have
`jd_tries=0`. The money half of that is closed (refuted rows cannot spend), but they still
take the walk ahead of rows with no text at all. Both are in `docs/BACKLOG.md`.

**The numbers it caught.** "43 of 197" pairs a denominator from one commit with a numerator
from another and never existed. The honest figures, on ONE population: of the 190 rows the
driver walks that pass the bar, **39** carry no strict echo and **38** become candidates.
(A later pass of the same wave caught me doing it again — "42 of 199 counting superseded"
paired a not-superseded numerator with a superseded denominator; it is 43 and 42 there.)
`_pane_denies_role`'s docstring said "36 of 186",
which matched no store on this branch. Both corrected everywhere they appear.

## CI caught what neither wave did: two of my tests were calling the live model

The first push (`52d51e8`) went red on the `guard` job — **1 failed, 1,782 passed** — with
`test_only_the_echo_suspicion_refutes_and_the_cheaper_ones_never_do :: assert (0 == 1)`. It
passes on this machine and fails on the runner, which is the difference the repo's own
pre-push contract exists to expose.

The cause is the trap the 2026-08-31 session wrote down and I read this morning:
**`enrich_matched_jd` imports `jd_quality` into its own namespace** (line 43) and calls the
LOCAL name at `_quality_pass`, so `monkeypatch.setattr(jdfill, "jd_quality", …)` never
reaches it. My test patched `jdfill` only. Locally the model answers, so the assertion held
for the wrong reason; on the runner the seam is auth-refused, `jd_quality` returns `None`,
the candidate is booked `unavailable` and no verdict is reached — `rejected` is 0, not 1.

So that test was **making a live model call on the public repo's CI**, which is the same
class of defect (if not the same magnitude) as the 43 escaped monkeypatches wave C found on
08-31. A second test had the same hole harmlessly: its `_boom` guard — which asserts the
model is never called on a cache hit — was installed where the call does not resolve, so it
could not have fired.

All three now patch `emj.jd_quality`, the name `_quality_pass` actually calls, which is the
convention the file's two older tests (`:18453`, `:19120`) already followed. Proved by
running the jd subset AND the full suite with `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`
and the CLI path emptied — the runner's condition.

**Not changed, deliberately:** the 08-31 session moved `fetch_jd`/`plain_fetch`/
`wayback_snapshot` to `jdfill.<name>` calls for exactly this reason and left `jd_quality`
behind. Making it consistent would break the two existing tests that patch `emj.jd_quality`,
so the inconsistency stays and is written down here instead.

## The interlock, because `looks_like_jd` is a shared predicate

Changing a predicate eleven call sites depend on is the shape that breaks other lanes
silently, so the veto was checked against every one of them rather than argued about:

**Verdict flips over the whole corpus (4,684 texts, old bar vs new): 0.** The single text
that flipped is the row this session repaired, so after the repair the veto changes nothing
anywhere — which is what a guard against recurrence should look like.

And per caller, if a flip ever does happen (`True -> False`):

| caller | what a flip does |
|---|---|
| `roles.better_description` | a non-JD LOSES to a JD, so the real text wins |
| `roles`' `description_quality` | publishes `snippet` instead of `jd` — more honest, not less |
| `seniority.cache_keys` | the key moves `\|jd` -> `\|bare`, orphaning the old verdict; a later `bare`->`jd` upgrade re-judges. No verdict is ever served for text the model did not see |
| the enrich todo | the row ENTERS the todo and is re-fetched — the intended effect |
| `_store_text` | its first clause already refuses a non-JD over a JD |
| `enrich_scrape_jd.save` | same shape, same refusal |
| `jdfill.actionable` | read-only selector; a flip only ADDS a row to a todo list |

**No caller blanks or shortens a description on a `False` verdict** — that was the one shape
worth fearing (it is how `roles.better_description` wrote `""` into both stores on 08-28),
and it does not exist. `grep -n "looks_like_jd" pipeline/*.py *.py` is the check.

## The stale verdicts, deleted with a declaration

Fixing a row's text does NOT re-judge it in general, and the brief's assumption that it does
is true only for a `bare`->`jd` upgrade: `seniority.cache_keys` builds
`<contract>|<company>|<title>|<jd|bare>` and carries **no hash of the description**. Three of
the four repaired rows changed text jd->jd, so their keys stayed byte-identical and the
verdict reached on the WRONG posting would have been served for ever.

`_versioned` finds a superseded verdict by SUFFIX, so an invalidation has to delete every
contract prefix, not just the current one. Deleted from `llm_cache` (`classifier`'s table --
declared here, in `HANDOFF.md` and in `546`):

    v2|techbiz global|data analyst|jd                          (was 1)
    v3.da2cb878|techbiz global|data analyst|jd                 (was 0)
    v3.7cb6831f|techbiz global|data analyst|jd                 (was 0)
    v3.da2cb878|ballerine|ai fraud data analyst (senior)|jd    (was 0)
    v3.7cb6831f|ballerine|ai fraud data analyst (senior)|jd    (was 0)
    v3.da2cb878|prisma photonics|senior product analyst|jd     (was 1)   <- the Data-Engineer text
    v3.7cb6831f|prisma photonics|senior product analyst|jd     (was 1)   <- the same

Ballerine and Prisma now have no cached verdict at all and are judged fresh on the correct
text. TechBiz keeps ONE row I deliberately did not delete: the pre-contract legacy key
`techbiz global|data analyst` = 0. `_lookup` falls through to it, and it returns
`judged_with_text=False`, which makes the row DRAINABLE (`not prior[3] and has_text`) -- so
the drain re-judges it, and until it does the stale value keeps the row EXCLUDED, which is
the safe direction. Deleting another lane's legacy row for no gain is not this lane's call.

`madanes` and `בנק דיסקונט` needed no deletion: the first is still text-less, and the second
had no cached verdict under its own key, so its new 1,819 characters are judged fresh.
