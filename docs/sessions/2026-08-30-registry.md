## `covered-by-row`: the name is not the identity, the board is

The operator found it: the queue held `Faye`, `queue-drain` resolved it to a Comeet board and
named the row after the board's **URL slug**, `withfaye`. The queue never credited `Faye` and
counted it as owed for two days while its roles published on the board.

Nothing had to be guessed. `Faye`'s own attempt log already said it:

```json
{"rung": "hunt", "verdict": "found", "url": "https://www.comeet.com/jobs/withfaye/87.00A"}
```

against a registry row holding Comeet uid `87.00A`. The uid is why this needs the shared
reader rather than a string compare: the registry stores `careers-api/2.0/company/<uid>/positions`
and the rung records `jobs/<slug>/<uid>`, so no comparison of the two URLs sees one board.
`apply_proposals.COMEET_UID` reads both, and `_url_keys` gives the url and host+path keys.

**Only a verdict that ASSERTS the page is this company's may credit** (`found`, `documented`,
`no-listing`, `resolved-domain`). Of the 20 raw board matches, six carried
`another company's board` — evidence about our SEARCH, never about the company, as
`DISPOSE_SYSTEM` says in as many words — and crediting them would have retired
`SMARTGEN WEALTH MANAGEMENT` onto Morgan Stanley's board, `Zaga1` and `Getsaucedelivery` onto
Just Eat Takeaway's, and `Action Item Software Ltd` onto Priority Software's.

**Name containment is not used at all.** It proposes `Intelligent Business`→`Intel`,
`Lumen`→`Lumenis`, `Welocalize`→`Localize`, `Siemens Energy`→`Siemens`,
`Beresheet Mobile Services`→`T-Mobile` — 33 such pairs on this queue. That is a HOLD for a
human, which is the rule `apply_proposals._name_kin` already records for new rows. Only two
name-based credits were taken, both EQUALITY after a normaliser the repo already trusts:
`Guideline Group`→`Guideline` (one trailing suffix) and `Meckano`→`mećkano` (a diacritic fold).

## A wrong retirement is reversible — proved, not asserted

There was no way back before today: the verdict lived in `queue_disposition.json`, `--dispose`
skipped any name whose `raw_verdict` is in `RETIRABLE`, and re-adding a name by hand only fed
it to the next cleanup. A retirement nothing can reverse is a deletion wearing a verdict.

`--reopen "<name>" --why "..." --apply` rewrites the record to `overturned-<verdict>`, keeps the
whole original under `overturned_from`, re-queues the name, and appends a `reopened` attempt
that lets it past the drain's 14-day cadence **once**. Compared by POSITION in the append-log,
not by date — a guard caught that both attempts are stamped to the day, so a same-day re-open
and re-search compare equal and the name would have stayed admitted for ever.

Proved end to end on real data: nine names re-opened, then `--retire-settled --apply` ran and
took the queue 284 -> 226 — **41 re-retired, not 42, because `Bulwarx` was one of the nine**.
All nine survive, all nine are in `queue_resolve_search.targets()` for tonight's cron, and every
disposition record still holds the evidence it was judged on.
