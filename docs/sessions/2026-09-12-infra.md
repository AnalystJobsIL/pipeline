Two items. Both same-day, A first. Three premises in the spawn brief turned out to be false and
three of mine did too, and the corrections are most of what this session is. Every number below
was re-derived here from committed state, from `gh api …/jobs/<id>/logs`, or from
`cloud_state/bd_spend.jsonl` and `cloud_state/source_health.json`.

**Credits spent: 0.** Every measurement is committed state or a run log; every new guard stubs
the first rung. What the change *costs* is in section 6 below.

## 1. Correction 1 — the day's discovery was not lost. Only the log was.

The brief says "Today's LinkedIn/Indeed discovery is LOST" and I repeated it before checking.
Committed state says otherwise: that run's own commit `71aff56` carries `discovered_cache.json`
**+5,398/−1,021** and `cloud_state/source_health.json`
`linkedin: {last_run: "2026-09-12", last_count: 4888}`. `sources.record` is the last call in
`main()`, so `main()` completed.

What died was all 52 `print` lines. The mechanism is legible in the *previous* day's log, where
every discovery line carries the single timestamp `09:31:33` — one flush, at exit, because
python block-buffers stdout when it is not a terminal.

This is the worst possible ordering of a failure: a capability that **produced** could not be
verified by what it produced (`CLAUDE.md` rule 1), and the step's own budget is what deleted the
proof.

## 2. Correction 2 — `timeout-minutes` does not kill the process. It abandons it.

The step was cancelled at **09:34:06**. `discovery_daily.py`'s `atexit` Bright Data ledger line
is stamped **09:37:46** — 3 m 40 s of life past its own cancellation, and in that window it wrote
`research_companies.json`. The *telegram* step, which GitHub started at 09:34:06, wrote the
**same file** at 09:34:10 (`queue: dropped 1 … drained 16 … 703 unresolved remain`).

**Two writers on the intake queue, in two different workflow steps.** §2's single-writer rule and
`CLAUDE.md` rule 4 both hold *inside* a process and neither reaches *across* steps, so this class
cannot exist until a step overruns — and then it is invisible, because the step that loses the
race reports success. It is the most serious thing found today and it has nothing to do with
LinkedIn.

The repo already owned the fix and was using it three steps away (`firmo_drain`) and in
`tests.yml` since 2026-08-30: `timeout --signal=INT --kill-after=30 22m python …`. The process
dies, the rc names itself, and 124/137 still reaches the mail through
`WORKFLOW_STEP_OUTCOMES` — which is the only reason the operator heard about any of this.

**Two neighbouring invariants in the same file were already false.** The step timeouts summed to
**313** against `timeout-minutes: 285`, while the comment claimed **270** *and* claimed the cap
sat above the sum "so the persist/outcome steps are reached by a step failing, never by the job
being cancelled from under them (BACKLOG 128)" — so a run that used every budget would have been
cancelled from under `persist`, the step that commits the LLM verdicts and the caches the run
paid for. Latent, not live (32 min on 08-25, 67 on 09-12). And **four steps carried no
`timeout-minutes` at all** (`stamp_enrich`, `mark_sent`, `gate`, `census`), which is the other way
a sum stops being a bound; each is seconds of work and now has 5 minutes. The only budget guard
in the repo read `tests.yml`, which is not the file that mails anything.

## 3. Correction 3 — the pacing WORKS, and deleting it would have been the regression

The brief's instruction was to delete the per-page pause unless it measurably prevents the 429,
and my own first reading agreed: over the five *unpaced* nights the blocked COUNT is a flat
**34–36** (35/365, 34/276, 36/250, 34/316, 34/334 = 9.6–14.4 %) across a **1.46×** swing in
request volume, which looks exactly like a quantity set by the query count rather than the
request rate — i.e. like something a 2.5-second gap cannot move.

That is a sound reading of those five nights. The sixth refutes it, and both of its measurements
survive in tracked files even though the log did not:

| night | pacing | `source_health` linkedin urns | `bd_spend` `discovery_daily.py` credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-28 … 09-11 (16 nights) | off | 1,400 – 3,016 | 17 – 23, **every one** |
| **2026-09-12** | **on** | **4,888** | **6** |

Indeed buys 5 of those credits nightly (5 queries × 1 unlock), so **LinkedIn's paid pages fell
from ~12–18 to ~1** while the free rung read **62 %** deeper than on its best previous night. Day
of week does not explain it: the previous Saturday, 09-05, unpaced, cost 21 credits for 1,999
urns. The mechanism is in the walk's own code — an unpaced walk earns 200-empty soft-limit pages,
breaks on `blanks >= LINKEDIN_BLANK_TOLERANCE`, and then **buys** a page to tell an empty keyword
from a rate limit. The free rung was buying the paid one twice over.

Written into the guard's docstring and the decision record because it is the conclusion the next
session will reach from the same five rows: **that number may only move with a number beside it.**

## 4. What was actually broken: a page count cannot bound a walk that sleeps

On 09-11's own counters (`free=302 blank=28 blocked=35` = 365 guest requests over 27 queries):
338 charged pauses × 2.5 s = **845 s**, plus 18–27 block re-asks × 20 s = **360–540 s**, on a
252 s sweep = **24.3–27.4 min against a 25-minute step**. Arithmetic, not weather.

The comment I wrote yesterday asserted "2.5 s x 50 pages x 9 keywords is inside the step's
25-minute budget". `_li_queries()` produces **27** queries, not 9, and even 9 × 49 × 2.5 =
18.4 min is not inside a 25-minute step that already works for 4.

So: `LINKEDIN_TIME_BUDGET_MIN` = **18**, read in `main()` and anchored at `main()` **entry** so
Indeed and Workable *compose* with it rather than adding to it (a pathological head starves the
walk, which is the safe direction). Past the deadline a query makes **no guest request** and goes
straight to the paid render; a query **already walking** stops and **buys nothing**.

**My first sketch was wrong three ways and each one matters.** `if spent(): cards, ok = [], False`
at the top of the loop would have (a) counted a request never made as `linkedin_blocked`, which is
the numerator of the one rate that decides the pause's fate; (b) printed `BLOCKED by LinkedIn` for
a block that never happened — the 2026-08-25 false-tripwire defect pointing the other way; and
(c) **spent money**: there is deliberately no `elif out: break` in the loop, so a mid-walk
fall-through buys `pages` renders to re-read a pool the free rung had just read. A clock must
never convert a productive free walk into spend. The fix is a sticky `starved` flag whose `i`
test *is* "this query has made no request yet".

`_blocked_reask` was **never reset anywhere** — not in `main()`, not in the test helper — so the
block re-ask was once per *process* and any guard over it depended on test order.

## 5. Intake gets a line in the mail (BACKLOG 180, closed)

`discovery` was the only step in the eight-step flow with no stamp, which is why a 25-minute
silent timeout reached the operator as a bare `workflow step 'discovery' failure` and nothing
about what the sweep had read. It is now the first entry of `pipeline/stages.ORDER`, stamped
beside `sources.record`, with `stages.alarms("discovery")` in `run.py`. **A run log expires; a
stamp is committed state**, which is what makes tomorrow's morning check answerable from the repo
rather than from a log GitHub will delete. Not done as 180 proposed: no separate
`- **Intake:**` line (that is `render`'s surface, and `Stage order:` renders every key), and
`queue_depth`/`bd_pct` are omitted because `queue` and `bd` are stages of their own.

The sweep line also gains the two things it lacked: a blocked **rate with its denominator**
(`blocked 35/365 guest requests (10%)`) and what the budget cost (`free walk served N of 27
queries, M cut mid-walk, K straight to paid`).

## 6. Item B — the caps, and the two corrections under them

The 09-12 mail had the BD cap binding at 30 and the Indeed cap at 25 with 15 postings judged on a
SERP snippet, while the **same library's other pool** — the 12:30 archive fill, for text nobody
publishes tonight — gets `JD_ENRICH_BD_CAP=1000` per run.

**Correction 4: demand is ~59/night, not the ~92 I derived.** The Indeed cards are a *subset* of
"postings the free rungs could not read", not an addend — `jdfill` increments both
`refused[(platform,"auth-walled")]` and `bd_unavailable_work` for the same card. The clean
readings are the two nights the ceiling switched the rung fully off: **53** on 09-10 (44 Indeed),
**49** on 09-11 (41), and 40 attempts + 15 auth-walled = **55** on 09-12. p95 **59** and **44**.

**Correction 5: `jd-fill 5/day` in the gauge is an artefact one day old.** `_book_jd_fill()`
shipped on the evening of 09-11, so the ledger labels nothing before it: 0/day for 09-01…09-10,
then 25 and 38. The honest version is stronger — the rung spent **its whole cap on 6 of the last
8 nights**, and on the other two the ceiling had switched it off.

**Correction 6: the cap is not the bound that binds next.** `Unlocker.__call__` defaults to
`timeout=90` and `_failing_at = max(breaker*2, min(breaker*4, max(3, cap//2)))` is 15 at cap 30
but **saturates at 20 for any cap ≥ 40**, so a night where every paid call times out is
20 × 90 s = **30 min** — already 90 % of a 25-minute budget *before* anything was raised. And the
pipeline step's budgets are **additive**, because `JDFiller` and `seniority.Classifier` interleave
in one loop: 8 + 25 + 60 + 15 + 2 = **110 against `timeout-minutes: 110`**, zero margin, with the
step's own comment omitting the jd-fill term.

So four numbers moved together: `JDFILL_BD_CAP` 30 → **150**, `JDFILL_INDEED_CAP` → **60** (set
explicitly; it was an invisible default for twelve days), `JDFILL_TIME_BUDGET_MIN` 25 → **35**,
step 110 → **130** (and the job cap to 360 to keep the sum under it).
`test_the_inline_jd_caps_bind_before_the_clock_and_the_clock_before_the_kill` derives all three
bounds from the workflow and the real `Unlocker`, so the next raise cannot skip one.

**Spend this change authorises:** the jd-fill rung goes from ~30 credits a night (cap-bound) to
an expected ~59, i.e. ~1,770/month against a 5,000 free tier — about **$2.65** at PAYG past it.
Commit 3's budget can route up to 9 national queries to the paid render on a starved night: 18
credits worst case, which is the ceiling `plan_spend` already advertises.

## 7. Two defects of my own, both found by running things rather than reading them

  of by the four that test it. Opt-in now.

guard bites, on a file holding **all** of commit 3's uncommitted test work. Everything replayed
from the scratchpad patch scripts, which is the only reason it was recoverable. **Commit before
proving a guard kills, not after.**

The heredoc trap fired twice as well (`\n` inside a `<<'EOF'` block becomes a real newline, and a
