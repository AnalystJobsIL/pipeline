## Three mechanisms, and the one that is not a fix

### M1 — a served-stale NO counted as the run's own reject (`a87aa8c`)

`seniority._classify` returns `decision: reject` for a verdict it did not make: a cache hit
under a RETIRED contract comes back as `path: llm_cache`, `contract: prior[4]`, reason
"cached LLM verdict (superseded contract)". `roles.reject_map` took any
`decision == "reject"`. Since 09-18 that cell is a **withdrawal**, so the map had to mean
"the classifier said NO *today*" and did not.

`reject_map(jobs, contract=clf.contract)` now skips a `_class` whose `contract` is not the
run's, which covers a retired prefix and the legacy `""`. It reads the `contract` KEY and
never the reason string — `_class_of`'s own rule, and one 09-02 commit rewrote 13 reasons.
The keyword head and a paid `llm` verdict both stamp `self.contract`, so neither moves; an
`llm_cache` hit under the LIVE contract still counts, because that is the live contract's
answer.

**The ACCEPT direction is deliberately untouched.** After this, a served-stale NO leaves the
cell as it was — which keeps a published row standing on a verdict no longer current. That is
`543`'s own gap for the subclass and it is the right side to fail on: **73 published cells
already name a non-live contract (43 of them `closed`)**, against three wrong deletions. The
real fix is `648`, and it belongs to `classifier`.

### M2 — a same-run rename orphaned the backfill map (`a87aa8c`)

`class_backfill.candidates` reads `ledger.records` at `run.py:553`. `fold_aliases` renames
records at 636. `record_run` applies the maps at 948. So the maps are keyed on the names the
ledger had BEFORE the fold: `self.records.get("phoenix financial|business analyst 50400095")`
returned None and the `accept` was dropped **silently** — no counter, no alarm — leaving the
stale `reject` cell for `_withdraw_rejected`.

`Ledger.renamed` had held `{old: new}` since `585` and its only reader was `flush`.
`Ledger._current_id` follows it to a fixed point (8 hops, so a cycle a future writer
introduces cannot hang the digest) and both loops resolve through it. `by_key` is refused
under **both** spellings, so a live accept wins whichever side of the fold the run's own jobs
were folded to. Counter: `re-keyed N` on the `Roles:` line.

### M3 — nothing could undo a machine withdrawal (`6dfb98c`)

`626` was closed on 09-18 on the strength of `_withdraw_rejected`'s reversal arm existing.
The arm is real; the pool that feeds it was not. `class_backfill.candidates` required
`status in PUBLISHED`, and a withdrawal is not published — so the reverse arm was reachable
only through a live `by_key` accept, and §7c's "reversible by the verdict alone" was false
for every record the run does not fetch. All three rows above were unreachable by any writer
in the system.

`class_backfill.re_offerable` is the missing pool, and every clause of it is a refusal:

* the machine's own withdrawals only (`withdrawn_by == "classifier"`). A hand line is a
  human's adjudication and `purged` is out for a reason no verdict touches;
* `reject_owed` still holding — the same one-way, self-draining predicate the published pool
  uses, so a withdrawal whose cell already names the live contract has left for good;
* and the live contract not already saying NO. `Madanes` and `Play Perfect` went out on the
  same sweep with genuine live-contract `|jd` NOs bought that run; re-offering them buys the
  same two calls every night for ever, which is exactly the cost `candidates` was narrowed to
  avoid on 2026-08-31 (9 of 42 candidates, 21 % of the pass, for cells no reader can see). A
  `|jd` YES is the authority and beats a `|bare` NO, per the split `reject_owed` documents.

`backfill:` counts it apart from the published refills — `N a machine withdrawal owed one` —
because a refill is a row IN the file carrying a NO nobody can check and a re-offer is a row
the machine has already taken out.

**And the `--dry-run` listing crashed on its own first pool.** Two of the three members carry
Hebrew, and `main`'s print was the one print in this seam not going through `_ascii`: it
raises `UnicodeEncodeError` on a cp1252 console **after** printing the first row, which reads
as "the pool is one row". Fixed in the same commit.

### Not a fix — the two-vote rule, REJECTED

A rule requiring two consecutive live NOs before a withdrawal would have changed the TIMING
for 2 of the 12 published reject cells (Play Perfect, Madanes) and the certain outcome for
**0 of 12**, while keeping a live-NO row public an extra day. Of the 8 machine withdrawals,
5 rest on exactly one live NO and 3 on zero; the three commits above close the three without
it. Orchestrator ruling, 2026-09-19. Play Perfect's adjudication goes to `649`.

## The mail clause named 2 of 16 (`a282774`)

The alarm was one `"; ".join(...)[:400]`. This was the first morning the count went above
one, and 400 characters bought **two** rows — a 470-character clause, the second's reason cut
mid-word — while fourteen companies went unnamed in the one place a human reads daily. A reason
is 120-250 characters of the classifier's prose; the names are the part only this clause
carries, since the reasons are all in `roles.csv.meta.json`'s `removed`.

`roles.withdrawn_clause` takes `(name, reason, by)` tuples from both append sites. If the
full `name — reason` join fits, the output is **byte-identical** to before — every ordinary
morning, and the two existing tests that pin the one-row shape. Otherwise it goes names-only
and groups by AUTHOR inside the ONE alarm (the orchestrator's ruling: the head
`roles withdrawn N role(s)` stays byte-identical so nothing downstream reads a new shape),
each group cut at a whole name with ` +N more`. Replayed over the real 09-19 withdrawals — a
**3,172-character** join — the two groups need **329** and **273** characters, so all 16 are
named and neither is cut.

Same commit: the meta's `excluded.note` said a withdrawn row "is listed under `withdrawn`".
The key is `removed`.
