Both the orchestrator and this lane read the caches, saw them thin, and concluded the layer

`JDFiller.maybe_fill` writes into the job dict, which reaches `matched` — **never back into

why both of us misread it.

**"Four green mornings that filled nothing" is false of this layer**, and it is my commit
message from yesterday. It was only ever true of the two BACKFILL drivers, which filled 6/0/1/0
over the same mornings. Corrected in §7a with both figures and with which mechanism fills what.

## The change: the inline filler may buy, bounded

This reverses a rule `JDFiller`'s own docstring carried — "never through Bright Data, the
backfills own that" — and the reason is that **the backfills cannot own this**. A posting whose
description decides its verdict has to be filled BEFORE the classifier runs, and a role the
classifier rejects on a bare title never reaches `matched` for `enrich_matched_jd` to find.
This is the only rung that runs at the right moment.

* free rungs first, always; the paid one sees only what they could not read
* `JDFILL_BD_CAP` (default `INLINE_BD_CAP` = 25) bounds it; `JD_BD=0` disarms it
* the summary line carries `Bright Data N/M filled (K credits)`, and paid fetches that fill
  nothing raise a bold `Stages:` clause — the rule `alarm_for` already applies to the backfills

**End to end on ten real failing postings from that class: 9 of 10 filled**, 7 on the free
rungs (LinkedIn answers differently per request) and 2 of the 3 handed to the paid rung, for
**1 credit**. Cost at 23 new failures a night: ~700 credits a month against the 5,000 that
begins 2026-09-01, and it is a cap rather than a target — a description that reaches `matched`
is not fetched again.

One inefficiency, deliberate and noted: the paid attempt re-runs the whole ladder, so a failed
posting costs a second free GET. It is harmless, it gives LinkedIn a second chance for nothing,
and removing it would mean threading the failed body through `fetch_jd`.

## Does it flow without a session? NO — and that is this lane's real remaining work

The operator's standard is not "the queue reached zero", it is "this flows automatically in git
from now on". Named concretely:

| | |
|---|---|
| **workflow** | `daily-digest.yml`, the `Run the pipeline` step -> `pipeline.run` -> `JDFiller`. No workflow edit is needed to reach the code: `JDFiller` self-arms from `JDFILL_BD_CAP`, default 25 |
| **cadence** | daily, 05:00 UTC nominal — and GitHub drifts it by hours (11:32Z on 08-29) |
| **alarm** | `inline jd-fill: N paid fetches filled 0` when credits buy nothing, and `inline jd-fill: the paid rung is configured and UNUSABLE (<why>) with N postings the free rungs could not read` when it cannot spend at all. Both reach the mail's bold `Stages:` line |

**And the rung is inert in production right now.** `daily-digest.yml`'s `Run the pipeline` step
passes `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN`, `SERPAPI_KEY` and `JDFILL*` — and **not**
`BRIGHTDATA_API_KEY` / `BRIGHTDATA_ZONE`. So the Unlocker constructs with
`unavailable="no-key"` and buys nothing. The two enrich steps immediately above it in the same
file DO carry those lines, which is why the backfills could spend and this rung cannot.

**So: until `infra` applies the two-line diff in BACKLOG 448, nothing about the LinkedIn class
is automatic.** The number moved on my machine — 9 of 10 — and it will not move on a runner.
I found this by reading the workflow rather than by watching a run fail, and the second alarm
above exists so that the next reader does not have to: the first digest after this lands will
say the rung is configured and unusable, in the mail, rather than reporting a quiet 23.

What IS automatic without any diff: the free rungs, which fill ~130 a night and always have.
