## 2. `WAYBACK_TIMEOUT_S` was applied twice a job, so 240 meant 480 (`44e4bf7`)

The 09-17 run (`35251054872`, `event: schedule`, `bacf07c`) measured:

| reading | value |
|---|---|
| worker-seconds | 3 workers × 3,733 s = **11,199** |
| what they bought | **34 jobs** of a 250 day cap, **15 captures** — 329 worker-s a job, 747 a capture |
| the hand run of 09-16, for contrast | ~**23 s** a job of wall clock |
| per-job wall time, estimated from the ledger's `at` gaps over 3 workers | median 250 s, **p90 305 s**, max 348 s |
| the ceiling those are measured against | `WAYBACK_TIMEOUT_S` **240** |

A p90 above the ceiling is the finding. `timeout` went to `_json_call` for the POST **and** to
`_poll` as its deadline, so 240 was a 480-s per-job ceiling and the `620` arithmetic was wrong
by a factor of two. The night's last ledger line landed at 18:12:24Z, **62 minutes into a
60-minute budget** — and a step that overruns `timeout-minutes` is ABANDONED, not stopped
(2026-09-12).

Shipped: the POST and the reads share ONE budget (`t0`), the POST taking at most half of it;
`_Pool._job_budget()` lowers that budget to the day's REMAINING clock over the workers, so no
address can outlive the step; `WAYBACK_TIMEOUT_S` **240 → 180** in the workflow (a 90-s POST —
57 % of the POSTs on 09-16 answered inside 30 s — and the reads after it). 60 jobs a night at
the ceiling, ~90 at the ~120 s a job that ends normally costs.

**What I did NOT do, and why.** A POST cut off at its ceiling is still `net:TimeoutError` and
is still retried once. `pending` for a cut-off POST was considered and refused for the second
time: a POST that never answered carries no `job_id`, so `pending` would route the address to
the availability API and, unanswered, to `unverified` — a refusal of the address (the 09-16
design rule). Suppressing the retry was drafted and dropped: with the POST at half the budget
a retry costs another half, which is the same per-address total, and dropping it would have
rewritten a test that pins the retry for a reason.

**`pending` is not a lost address any more.** All 8 of the 09-17 `pending` lines carry a
`job_id`, as do all 14 of the 09-16 ones, and the verify loop asks `/save/status/<job_id>`
first: **7 of the 09-16 pendings came back `verified` on 09-17**, none `unverified`. The 09-17
morning row asked for **0** `pending`; on the keyed rung that was the wrong question, and the
09-20 row asks the right one.

**The 60-minute budget stays.** A 120-minute step was rejected on 243 s a job against the hand
run's 23: a second hour buys jobs only once a job costs what it should, and the step must never
reach the 19:00 listing-hunt slot.

## 4. `614`(b): the hidden-role alarm names the title (`43e2bc9`)

role over `cloud_state/roles.jsonl` and both caches — and "fix the scrape" had been sending the

keeps `N hidden: mangled title`, which the board footer and the audit line pin, so no rendered
string that anything asserts has moved.

## 7. Clause 4

**Deleted or unified.** The second full `timeout` inside `_submit_job` (one budget, two
consumers); the `email_hidden` counter and the board's `hidden` counter became one thing (the
cards), with `len()` where a number is wanted; `report`'s `hidden=0` int parameter.

**Extended, not duplicated.** `_Pool` grew `_job_budget()` and `_submit` reads it — the pool
already owned the clock (`_exhausted`, `slot`), so the bound lives beside them;
`STATUS_EXT` gained no entry, one existing entry changed class; `rolecard.report` kept its
signature position and changed what `hidden` IS; `tests.yml`'s existing calibration comment
grew a dated block instead of being replaced.

**Rejected, on the number.** A 120-minute archive step (243 s a job vs 23); narrowing the
tiers to dodge Indeed and LinkedIn (5.2 % of targets, not 46.5 %); parking `www.linkedin.com`
(the hand POST's answer was the archive's state, not the address's); a SERP zone for
google.com (`630`: **one** open Google Israel record); wiring `_body_class` into the `error`
path (it would park the host that captures 47 of 64); 14 mutation shards (~1,922 s, over the
line before the next push); suppressing the retry after a cut-off POST (the same per-address
total once the POST has half the budget).

**Made harder.** `_Pool._job_budget` is a second place a per-job wait can be wrong, and a test
that sets `timeout_s` now also sees `workers` and `time_min` in the number the rung receives;
`rolecard.report`'s `hidden` is a sequence — a caller passing an int gets a truthy value and a
`len()` crash; `STATUS_EXT["bad-request"]` is a 30-day park, so a host that starts answering
`bad-request` for a transient reason waits a month; twenty CI jobs where there were eight, and
GitHub's concurrency cap means the gate now runs in two waves.
